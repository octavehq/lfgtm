# proposal: task method

Resolve whether the user wants a seller working draft, a completed buyer proposal, or a collaborative POC/success plan. A requested scaffold is a valid deliverable. For a completed proposal, use working placeholders while collecting missing details and continue until the requested content has been incorporated and reviewed.

Read the CRM opportunity, relevant calls, approved offer/implementation resources, and available agreements first. Extract confirmed scope, stakeholders, evaluation criteria, proposed versus agreed commitments, and next decision. Request only remaining inputs, for example: “The opportunity and call confirm the package and pilot scope. Please supply the approved price/term, feasible rollout milestones, and delivery owner; I will incorporate them and rerun the review.” CRM/call access does not imply that commercials or telemetry are present.

Track unanswered inputs across turns. Insert operator answers, update dependent sections and figures, check consistency with existing agreements, and rerender/review. Do not invent prices, contractual terms, dates, staffing commitments, or POC results. Show sourced standard phases as proposed until agreed; state dependencies and acceptance criteria.

For a POC/success plan, define the baseline, measurement method, success threshold, in-scope users/data/workflows, test period, measurement/approval owners, and the decision after the POC. Collect missing definitions through targeted questions. Future “measured result” and “final acceptance” fields may intentionally remain open with owner and timing. These are planning fields, not accidental unfinished seller inputs.

Start with the buyer's stated value driver and figures in CRM/calls. Ask for only missing calculation inputs: volume, frequency, baseline effort/cost, measured or expected change, adoption/ramp, investment and implementation cost, and reporting horizon. Identify which are measured, buyer-reported, operator assumptions, or future POC measurements.

For time savings, calculate eligible volume × time saved per unit × adoption; convert minutes to hours before applying a cost rate. Separate capacity released from cash savings, and require a realization mechanism for the latter. Separate one-time from recurring costs and avoid counting the same benefit twice. For positive total cost, ROI = (benefit − total cost) / total cost; otherwise mark ROI undefined. Show payback only when positive recurring net benefit repays the stated initial cost. Do not combine annual and monthly figures without conversion. Use scenarios when uncertainty materially changes the decision. Keep the decision summary concise and include an auditable assumption/calculation table. Later answers must update every dependent number.

Compare the actual alternative when it matters: incumbent vendor, internal build, manual work, or inaction. Explain evidenced business tradeoffs against the buyer's criteria; do not manufacture a named competitor. Use the strongest applicable approved proof, even when it is one qualitative result. Let the evidence determine the number/layout of proof cards. Attribute direct operator facts as such; do not require them to appear in a tool result.
