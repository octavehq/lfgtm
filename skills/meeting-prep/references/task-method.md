# meeting-prep: task method

The prep answers four questions for the rep, in this order: who has been in this exact situation before and what happened (lookalike deals), who will be in the room and what each person will likely raise (what to expect), what to say and bring, and what single move to make next. Everything else supports those four.

When rules conflict: accuracy and honest uncertainty first, then the issue that decides the next buyer decision, then style.

## Ground rules

- **Sources, strongest first:** transcripts and the records of this account (what happened); the deal record; outcome data from comparable deals; the seller's authored strategy (guidance, never facts about this account); extracted findings and summaries (pointers to moments that prove nothing by their absence). Check every finding you rely on against the call or email it came from.
- **Never invent** quotes, numbers, customers, proof, content, capabilities, roles, objections the buyer did not voice, attendees, or events. A gap is stated as what was checked and not found ("no earlier calls with this account were found"), never with a reason the data does not give.
- **Three voices stay apart:** what the buyer said, what the rep said, what the seller's strategy says. Strategy text is our guidance ("our answer when marketers say alerts are noisy is ..."), never observed data about this buyer or our deals.
- **Claims carry their strength** (seen on this deal, seen on comparable deals, compared, guessed). Label anything derived from comparable deals as *likely*. No causal verbs: comparable deals are told as sequence ("2 of 3 deals where the rep ran a same-data comparison moved to a pilot"), never as the reason a deal was won.
- **Counts carry their base.** "2 of 3" names what the 3 are ("3 won insurance deals with call records"), and a rate never appears without its count. Say how the comparable set was chosen. A pattern seen on one deal is told as one deal.
- **State facts, never how you got them.** The reader sees the fact with its date or source in plain words ("about 800 employees"; "call on Mar 20"), never the lookup: no "returned", "the graph", "retrieved", "the data shows", tool or field names, in any heading or table.
- **Other customers stay internal.** Lookalike deals are for the rep. Never put another customer's name, deal or numbers in wording the rep would say to the buyer, and say so in the section intro. A reference record is not permission to name a customer.
- **The seller's own records can disagree** (a proof point says in production, a launch brief says pilot). State both in one line, use the more cautious wording in anything the rep will say, and tell the rep to confirm which is current.
- **Dates and windows.** `list_events`, `list_findings`, `compare_cohorts` and `list_cohort_deals` default to a window of the last 14 days. Always pass an explicit `startDate` well before the deal was created (or before the earliest deal in the set) and an `endDate`, and give `list_cohort_deals` exactly the `cohortA`, `cohortB`, filters, window, `linkScope` and outcome you gave `compare_cohorts` so the counts agree. `list_events` takes `opportunityIds` inside `filters`. An empty list from a default window is not "no calls".
- **Tools the host does not expose.** If a tool named in these instructions is not available, skip that step, say once in the sources footer what was not checked, and continue with the narrow tools that are.
- **Derived figures and descriptors.** Quote a statistic (rate, interval, lift, gap) only as the tool states it, and do not compute ranges or intervals of your own; dates, day counts and call spans worked out from record dates are not statistics and are allowed, and do not describe a customer, deal or reference ("an education marketplace", "a hotel group") with words the record does not contain.
- **Roles** only as the data gives them; a derived role is labeled ("likely the budget holder").
- **Status words need a source.** Call a proof point, deck or claim "approved", "current" or "in production" only when the record says so (an active flag, a status field, a date); otherwise say "on file" and name what the record shows.

## 1. Gather

Resolve the meeting and the deal. The target can be an account, a contact, a deal id, or a calendar title; if several deals match, name the choice you made. Find the meeting itself before anything else (step 0 below), then infer type and duration from the invite, stage and history. Ask one grouped set only for missing purpose, participants, date, or recent changes that would alter the plan; continue with everything supported while waiting, and in a run nobody can answer, prepare for the next conversation with the primary contact and say once what was searched for the meeting and that no invite was found. Distinguish a new account with no interaction from a workspace access failure. When records disagree (a call linked to a different deal, a customer-versus-prospect label, a summary that names someone the transcript does not), state the conflict in one line in the context section and prefer, in order: the transcript, the deal's own linked activity, the CRM fields, the generated summary. When the buyer's calls and emails sit on a sibling deal at the same account (the deal is new and has none of its own), treat them as this account's history with this buyer, say which sibling deal they are attached to, and still prepare from them.

Read [lookalikes and expectations](lookalikes-and-expectations.md) and [strategy gap](strategy-gap.md) before selecting evidence; they hold the method for the two sections that make this prep different from a summary of the account.

Retrieval order. Run independent calls together.

0. **The meeting.** Look for the invite in three places, together: `list_events` by the account domain with `startDate` today and `endDate` a few weeks ahead (the default window looks back, so upcoming meetings are missed without future dates), with participants; the account's CRM meetings and tasks (`find_crm_records` for the account's record id, then `find_crm_activities` on that account record; calendar invites usually live here as meeting activities, and `get_deal` activity alone can miss them); and the email threads with the account, where scheduling and attendee changes often live. From the invite take the title, date, time and time zone, place and dial-in, organizer, every invited guest and who accepted. The invited guests are the room: never write that someone will "likely" attend when an invite exists, and list every guest. A person the emails add or mention who is not on the invite is listed as "added by email, not on the invite"; a first name alone in an email is resolved from the thread, the signature and the CRM contacts before you write that the role is unknown. When no invite is found, say once what was searched and the window ("no invite in calendar events Oct 26 to Nov 20, the deal's CRM meetings or the email threads"), per [information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md) rule 23.
1. **This account.** The deal (`get_deal` with activity and stakeholders), every call and email with the account (`list_events` by domain with participants, and `includeDeal` when you need the deal each event belongs to; page it and read only the fields you need), the latest call in full or by findings (`get_event_detail`), CRM activity. Employee count and segment from the deal's account. Read the summary and next-step fields of every recent call and email, especially the latest message from the buyer, and list the meetings they proposed and the asks still open before you write that nothing is booked or that no date exists; a proposed time that has already passed is stated as such ("proposed Thursday 2 PM, Mar 22, no confirmation"). Call a stretch of time quiet only after listing every event in it, internal syncs included. Read the deal's CRM tasks and notes and the email bodies for anything that changes how the room should be run: an advisor, investor or other commercial tie between our side and a buyer contact, an outside advisor who shapes the shortlist, a champion who is leaving. State it once, internally, in the context section with what it changes (for example, which buyer contact should carry the internal case), and keep it out of drafts and prompts that leave the company (rule 19). Fix the position in one sentence: stage, last touch, who has been engaged, who has not.
2. **Ask Octave, seeded.** One question per call, seeded with the deal (`crmo_`) and every entity id you have resolved (segment, persona, competitor, objection). Calls to make, in this order, each only if the one before leaves it open:
   - "What worked in deals like this one, and who was involved?" (lookalikes).
   - "What do [persona] buyers in [segment] raise at [stage], and which answers came before the deal moving?" (what to expect). One call per persona in the room.
   - "Which approved narratives, proof points and references appear on won deals in this segment or for this persona?" (content; skip when the strategy cell's linked elements already answer it).
   - "What does our strategy for this persona, segment and stage say to do, and which of that has not happened on this deal?" (strategy gap). Ask it even when no strategy cell exists for the combination: the answer then shows what the authored records say instead, and says so when it finds none.
   Check the `seeds` first: if the focal deal or persona it resolved is not the one you asked about, discard the answer and re-ask with the right ids. Trace any statement that says something happened on this account's calls to the finding or event it cites; if that item belongs to another account (or this account has no calls), discard the statement. Use the answer as a map. It is orientation, never a quote and never a statistic. Open every consequential item it cites with the narrow tool for its kind before using it, and use items marked inferred as *likely*. If the answer says it lacks something (participants, timing, outcomes), take that as the next narrow call, not as a finding. The responses can be very large (hundreds of thousands of characters, mostly inferred links): when a result is saved to a file, read only the `answer` and `seeds` and the first forty evidence names and rationales with a script, and open at most ten items. Treat any count in the answer that is not a deal count (associations, mentions, findings) as unverified: check it with `list_cohort_deals` or `list_deals` before quoting it, and drop a figure that cannot be checked or that looks like an artifact (every deal in a set).
3. **Verify with the narrow tools.** Cohorts and counts (`compare_cohorts`, `list_cohort_deals`), the deals behind them (`get_deal`, `get_event_detail` on their calls before they closed), exact words (`search_call_transcripts`, `get_entity_evidence`), authored guidance (`find_motion_icp` for the persona and segment cell, `get_entity` for the objection, proof point, use case, competitor, reference), and win and loss patterns (`deal_pulse_win_loss_cohort`, `get_competitive_insights`).
4. **Content.** The cell's linked proof points, use cases and references (`find_motion_icp` with elements; resolve the motion first with `list_motions`), approved narratives and decks (`search_resources`, `list_resources`), and how each performs (`get_entity` with usage).
5. **Framework.** Look for an authored pitch framework, talk-track structure or rubric in the resources and entities (see [meeting sections](meeting-sections.md), "What to say and ask"). The strategy cell's methodology focus is a sales methodology: it organizes the questions and goals and is not a pitch framework.

If a tool is unavailable or empty, name the gap once in the source notes and continue with the narrow tools. A missing graph pass is a reduced check, never a reason to stop.

## 2. Resolve the context (internal, not printed as a table)

1. **People.** Each attendee's title, persona and buying role, and which personas the strategy expects at this stage that are absent. Flag a title that contradicts the call. When no persona resolves for a title, say so, pick the nearest persona by buying role, and label it "nearest persona, low confidence"; never present it as the person's persona.
2. **Where the buyer is.** What they said they want and their next decision, in their words. Commitments either side made.
3. **What they use instead,** in their words; where it works (concede) and where it breaks (test).
4. **Lookalike set.** The closest won and lost deals and what happened on their calls before they moved or stalled.
5. **Expectations.** Per persona, what the persona raises at this stage in this segment and which answers came before deals that moved.
6. **Strategy gap.** What the strategy prescribes for this persona, segment and stage against what the evidence shows has happened on this deal.
7. **Content.** The specific approved narrative, slide material or proof that fits, with where it came from.
8. **Framework.** Present or absent.

## 3. Decide the few things that matter

1. **The job this meeting has,** in one sentence, with the buyer's next decision. Read the meeting's own setting first ([information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md) rules 16 to 22): who organized it, how many vendors or sessions it is one of, whether it is a first session, and the size of the sale. Every ask on the page fits that setting. At a multi-vendor pitch day or a first enterprise session the ask is the next step in the buyer's own process (a place on the shortlist, a follow-up session with the person who carries the case internally); weeks on the buyer's data, a pilot, a budget owner or a decision date wait for a sponsor and a later meeting, and appear at most as questions for later.
2. **Rank what you found by its effect on that decision.** Buyer admissions and stated pains, a request raised twice or unanswered, an earlier loss or silence, the reason to act now, an unanswered signal the strategy has a play for. Context that changes no line, row, question or move stays off the page.
3. **Pick the move.** One to three next best actions from the [strategy gap](strategy-gap.md). Zero is a valid answer.

## 4. Positioning

Select positioning from the actual offer and motion: complement an existing approach when coexistence creates value; replace when supported superiority justifies switching costs; consolidate when evidence supports replacing several tools; build-versus-buy when ongoing ownership is the buyer's alternative; service when delivery and expertise create the value. State the fit condition, prerequisites and tradeoff. Never assume every customer sells the same thing or achieves an unsupported multiplier. Load [positioning coach](positioning-coach.md) and [strategic coach](strategic-coach.md) only when choosing the approach.

For each goal, state the buyer belief or decision to explore, the evidence already established, what remains hypothetical, the question that could confirm or disconfirm it, and the next commitment if supported. Recognition is a joint test, not a predetermined conclusion.

## 5. One home per point, and length

Before writing, list the page's points: the deciding issue, the lookalike finding, the expected concern, the content pick, the move. Give each exactly one home. The short version names it, its section develops it, and every other section either adds something new (a quote, a date, a person, a line to say) or says nothing about it. A fact stated in the context section is not restated in goals, expectations and the next action. Length yields to the evidence rules: a shorter page is correct when the evidence is thin, and nothing is added to reach a size. Target about 2,500 words including tables, and not counting the email drafts or the keep-going prompts, for a 30-minute meeting; a thin account makes a shorter page. Budget it by section: short version 60, lookalikes 350 (table cells of 15 words or fewer), context 250, goals 250, competitive 200, what to expect 100 per person for at most five people (the other guests get one line each in the context section), content 200, say and ask 350, next best action 300. Count the words before delivering. When the page runs over, cut whole items, lowest ranked first (a goal, a lookalike row, an optional question, a third move); never meet the budget by shrinking type or clipping sentences into fragments. Questions that are only questions (who else is involved, how to frame a comparison) belong in the say-and-ask section, not in the next best action.

## 6. Say plainly when there is nothing to find

A thin or flat result is a finding. The plain form is "No [thing] found for [scope]." Use it once where it changes what the rep does (a table cell counts); the other empty cases go in the sources footer, with where you looked. Where the same call or contact is linked to both a won and a lost deal at an account, say the evidence cannot separate those outcomes. When the lookalike set is small, when no play separates the deals that moved from those that stalled, when no strategy cell exists for this persona and segment, when no framework or content fits, or when there is no call to read, write one plain line saying what you looked for and what you found (or leave the case to the sources footer when it changes nothing for the rep), then continue with what is supported. Do not manufacture a pattern, widen a cut silently, or borrow another account's pain. Starting from the nearest strategy cell is allowed when you say which cell and why (same persona in another segment first, then the same segment with the closest buying role), and treat its text as guidance for the kind of buyer, not for this one.

## 7. Before delivering

Keep an internal ledger while you work (never printed): one line per claim that will appear on the page, with the saved result that supports it. Build the page only from ledger lines. (a) Every per-deal detail in the lookalike table (outcome, date, segment, employee count, calls and span, competitor named, loss reason, who joined) comes from that deal's own record; an attribute you did not check for that deal is left blank or marked "not checked". (b) Describe every content item, proof point and reference only with words from the record you fetched; never infer what it covers from its name. (c) Tally every count from the listed rows, write the base beside it, and make the totals on the page agree with each other. (d) Never say a pattern is absent or "predicts nothing" when you have rates: give the rates with their counts. (e) Rewrite lookup phrasing as facts: not "I opened 3 won deals" but "3 of the won deals have call records"; no "on record", "the record shows", "I counted", "I read", "calls read" (say "calls with transcripts"), "computed in this run", ids as prose, or tool names in the body. (f) Work out every relative date and elapsed time from the two dates (created Mar 3, today Mar 5 is 2 days) and every "strategy says" line from the text of the strategy record, naming which record; a strategy source you cannot point to is cut. (g) Write conclusions for the rep, not cohort mechanics: say "the gap is small and not stable" with the two counts, never "link scope", "default view" or "the event list attaches". (h) Read every date, figure and name in the prose against the page's own tables and cards (a deal's pilot dates in a sentence match that deal's row), and fix the one that disagrees with its record ([information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md) rule 24). (i) Remove contrast constructions ("X, not Y", "not X but Y", "rather than", "instead of" used to contrast, "one deal, not a pattern") and state the one thing that is true. Ids and tool names are allowed only inside the keep-going prompts, which are written for an agent, and in the asset line of the keep-going block.

Then check the draft against these rules. In a headless host the checks go in a separate short message after the Markdown prep, or are omitted when the host allows one message only. The checks go in your chat reply, never in the document (the document has no "checks run" block and no account of how it was built; a short "sources" footer naming dates and kinds of source is enough). Report only the checks you ran: every quote and date looked up; every count has its base; every item from comparable deals is labeled likely and names no customer in buyer-facing wording; every content item exists in the library or resources; the framework section appears only if a framework was found; the room matches the invite and every ask fits the meeting's setting; prose agrees with the tables; the page is within its word budget; each next best action has all four parts; headings, vocabulary and punctuation follow [output rules](output-rules.md); ids and links match a result you saw.
