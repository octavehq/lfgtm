# Meeting content schema

Use one header, one snapshot, a three-line short version (the situation, what comparable deals say, the move), then these eight sections in order. The whole prep should read in about ten minutes: each section is tight, and a section with nothing supported is one plain line. IDs: lookalikes, context, goals, competitive, expect, content, say-ask, next-action. Navigation, outline and delivery summary derive from this list. A "Keep going" block follows the last section (see Delivery below). Interior structure and wording are given in [lookalikes and expectations](lookalikes-and-expectations.md) and [strategy gap](strategy-gap.md).

1. **Deals like this: what worked, what didn't** (`lookalikes`). Lead section, marked internal. The closest won and lost deals by segment, persona, competitor or concern. First line: how the set was chosen and its size. Then a table of at most six rows: deal (internal name), outcome, what they share with this deal, what happened on their calls before they moved or stalled. Then the contrast with counts and their base, as sequence, never as cause. Thin or empty set: say so first.
2. **Context.** The meeting as the invite gives it (date, time, place, organizer, every invited guest), or one line naming what was searched when no invite was found; company fit and limits; selected opportunity and latest commitments; any tie that changes how the room is run, stated once with what it changes. Keep persona pains out of factual company context unless confirmed.
3. **Goals.** Positioning directive; evidence-supported goals when warranted; each goal carries evidence status, why it matters, a confirming and a disconfirming question, and the conditional next commitment. Fewer goals are valid.
4. **Competitive.** The actual incumbent, evaluated competitor or status quo; buyer criteria; supported differences and concessions; what to verify. Add how deals against this competitor went when lookalike evidence exists, with counts. No invented incumbent or forced attack.
5. **What to expect, persona by persona** (`expect`). One block per invited guest who is likely to speak or decide, at most five; the other guests get one line each in the context section. Each block: persona and buying role, what they have said, what this persona typically raises at this stage in this segment (likely, with the lookalike count and a short quote), the answer that worked, and the question to ask. Observed concerns on this account come first; this section replaces a separate objections section.
6. **Content to use.** The specific approved narrative, slide material, proof point or reference to bring, each with why it fits this segment or persona, and where it won when the entity carries usage data (`get_entity` with usage; resources carry none, so write "no win data on file"), and what to leave out. Every item exists in the library or resources; name the resource and the passage you saw (some resources have no title: then name the source address or quote the opening words of the passage). Reps pull a few slides into their own deck, so say which parts, and only slide numbers or titles you can see. When a gap exists (the buyer needs proof we do not have), say so. Never invent a deck, slide, case study or number. Never name a reference customer in rep-facing wording; describe it only with words its record contains (otherwise "a customer in this segment") and tell the rep to confirm before naming it.
7. **What to say and ask** (`say-ask`). The opening, the argument, buyer-specific proof, grounding and branching questions, and an ask that fits the meeting's setting (a pitch day or first session asks for the next step in the buyer's process, never weeks on their data or a budget owner); spoken wording separate from the rationale. No minute splits or timings unless the buyer or the calendar gave them. **Pitch framework:** if, and only if, the workspace has authored a pitch framework, methodology or rubric (a resource or entity that names one; the strategy cell's methodology focus alone does not count, see below), structure this section as that framework's steps, using its own names, and say where it came from. If none was found, omit any framework language and do not label the section as framework-based; do not invent a framework, and do not treat a generic early, mid or late stage description as one. A workspace-wide sales methodology listed on the strategy cell (for example qualification steps) is a methodology, not a pitch framework: when only that exists, use its steps to organize the questions and the goals, say so and name where it came from, and leave the pitch itself unstructured.
8. **Next best action** (`next-action`). One to three moves, each with the move, why now, strategy source and follow-through (an email draft, a person to add, a document to send), then the meeting success condition, sized to what this meeting can achieve. Zero moves is a valid answer, stated plainly with the signal that would change it. This is the last section; the keep-going block and a short sources footer (dates and kinds of source) follow it.

Account facts, operator-reported facts and hypotheses must agree across headings, labels, intros and talk tracks. A persona or segment pain is a working hypothesis until account evidence supports it. Anything derived from other deals is labeled likely.

Health, family and personal-life details said on a call or noted in the CRM stay out of the page, the email drafts and the keep-going prompts ([information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md) rule 19); the delivery message reports how many were left out.

## Delivery: asset and continuing the conversation

No separate chat mode. The deliverable carries its own way back in, so a rep, an internal agent, Claude or ChatGPT can pick it up through the Octave MCP.

- **Identify the asset.** If the prep was published, give its asset id and URL (read back after publishing). If it was not, say it is unpublished and name the deal id, account domain and meeting date that identify it.
- **Keep going block.** After section 8, three to five prompt cards in the shape of [HTML document principle 9](../../shared/formats/html-document.md): a title, one line on what it produces and one "Copy prompt" button. The copied text is the card's task followed by one shared context block written once for all cards, so every copied prompt stands alone. The shared block carries the account and domain, deal id and stage, meeting date, attendees with roles as the data gives them, the asset UUID from the publish result (`asset_get_by_id` needs the UUID; if you only have a URL, give the URL and tell the agent to find the asset with `assets_list`) when there is one, what to produce, the key facts, and the instruction to fetch the asset (`asset_get_by_id`) or rebuild it from the deal, and to start with `ask_octave`; each card's task carries what to produce and anything specific to it. Typical prompts: draft the follow-up email for a chosen move; rehearse the call as a chosen persona using the expected concerns; go deeper on one lookalike deal; find and add the missing person; refresh the prep after the call. No transcript text and no other customer's name in a prompt that will leave the company: identify a lookalike deal in a prompt by its deal id only, never by account or deal name.
- **Headless hosts.** In a chat or agent host that cannot render a page, deliver the same eight sections as Markdown with the same order and labels, then the prompts. HTML is for hosts that can render it. Do not ask the user to open an Octave screen.
