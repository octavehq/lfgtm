# Meeting content schema
Use one header, one snapshot, and these six sections in order. IDs are context, goals, competitive, say-ask, objections, takeaway. Navigation, outline, and delivery summary derive from this list.

1. Context: company fit and limits; actual attendees versus suggested roles; selected opportunity and latest commitments. Keep persona pains out of factual company context unless confirmed.
2. Goals: positioning directive; two or more evidence-supported goals when warranted; recognition cards containing evidence status, why it matters, confirming/disconfirming probe, and conditional advancement. Fewer goals are valid when appropriate.
3. Competitive: the actual incumbent, evaluated competitor, or status quo; buyer criteria; supported differences and concessions; what to verify. No invented incumbent or forced attack.
4. What to Say & Ask: sequence the opening, argument, buyer-specific proof, grounding/branching questions, and next-decision ask. Separate spoken wording from seller rationale.
5. Objections: observed concerns first, relevant anticipated concerns labeled; distinguishing question; supported response and proof; genuine limit or unresolved commitment.
6. Takeaway: the primary move, meeting success condition, and next action/owner; intentional planning placeholders may remain if the operator chooses a working prep.

Account facts, operator-reported facts, and hypotheses must agree across headings, labels, intros, and talk tracks. A persona/segment pain is a working hypothesis until account evidence supports it. Preserve the existing detailed status-evidence definitions wherever compatible with this rule.
