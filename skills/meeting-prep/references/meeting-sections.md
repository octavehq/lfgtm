# Meeting content schema

Use one header, one snapshot, a three-line short version (the situation, what comparable deals say, the move), then these eight sections in order. The whole prep should read in about ten minutes: each section is tight, and a section with nothing supported is one plain line. IDs: lookalikes, context, goals, competitive, expect, content, say-ask, next-action. Navigation, outline and delivery summary derive from this list. A "Keep going" block follows the last section (see Delivery below). Interior structure and wording are given in [lookalikes and expectations](lookalikes-and-expectations.md) and [strategy gap](strategy-gap.md).

1. **Deals like this: what worked, what didn't** (`lookalikes`). Lead section, marked internal. The closest won and lost deals by segment, persona, competitor or concern. First line: how the set was chosen and its size. Then a table of at most six rows: deal (internal name), outcome, what they share with this deal, what happened on their calls before they moved or stalled. Then the contrast with counts and their base, as sequence, never as cause. Thin or empty set: say so first.
2. **Context.** Company fit and limits; actual attendees versus suggested roles; selected opportunity and latest commitments. Keep persona pains out of factual company context unless confirmed.
3. **Goals.** Positioning directive; evidence-supported goals when warranted; each goal carries evidence status, why it matters, a confirming and a disconfirming question, and the conditional next commitment. Fewer goals are valid.
4. **Competitive.** The actual incumbent, evaluated competitor or status quo; buyer criteria; supported differences and concessions; what to verify. Add how deals against this competitor went when lookalike evidence exists, with counts. No invented incumbent or forced attack.
5. **What to expect, persona by persona** (`expect`). One block per attendee: persona and buying role, what they have said, what this persona typically raises at this stage in this segment (likely, with the lookalike count and a short quote), the answer that worked, and the question to ask. Observed concerns on this account come first; this section replaces a separate objections section.
6. **Content to use.** The specific approved narrative, slide material, proof point or reference to bring, each with why it fits this segment or persona and where it won, and what to leave out. Every item exists in the library or resources; name the resource and the passage you saw (some resources have no title: then name the source address or quote the opening words of the passage). Reps pull a few slides into their own deck, so say which parts, and only slide numbers or titles you can see. When a gap exists (the buyer needs proof we do not have), say so. Never invent a deck, slide, case study or number. Name a reference customer in rep-facing wording only when its record says external use is allowed; otherwise describe it by type ("a consumer brand") and tell the rep to confirm before naming it.
7. **What to say and ask** (`say-ask`). The opening, the argument, buyer-specific proof, grounding and branching questions, and the next-decision ask; spoken wording separate from the rationale. No minute splits or timings unless the buyer or the calendar gave them. **Pitch framework:** if, and only if, the workspace has authored a pitch framework, methodology or rubric (the cell's methodology focus, or a resource or entity that names one), structure this section as that framework's steps, using its own names, and say where it came from. If none was found, omit any framework language and do not label the section as framework-based; do not invent a framework, and do not treat a generic early, mid or late stage description as one. A workspace-wide sales methodology listed on the strategy cell (for example qualification steps) is a methodology, not a pitch framework: when only that exists, use its steps to organize the questions and the goals, say so and name where it came from, and leave the pitch itself unstructured.
8. **Next best action** (`next-action`). One to three moves, each with the move, why now, strategy source and follow-through (an email draft, a person to add, a document to send), then the meeting success condition. Zero moves is a valid answer, stated plainly with the signal that would change it. This is the last section; the keep-going block and a short sources footer (dates and kinds of source) follow it.

Account facts, operator-reported facts and hypotheses must agree across headings, labels, intros and talk tracks. A persona or segment pain is a working hypothesis until account evidence supports it. Anything derived from other deals is labeled likely.

## Delivery: asset and continuing the conversation

No separate chat mode. The deliverable carries its own way back in, so a rep, an internal agent, Claude or ChatGPT can pick it up through the Octave MCP.

- **Identify the asset.** If the prep was published, give its asset id and URL (read back after publishing). If it was not, say it is unpublished and name the deal id, account domain and meeting date that identify it.
- **Keep going block.** After section 8, three to five copy-ready prompts, each stand-alone: account and domain, deal id and stage, meeting date, attendees with roles as the data gives them, the asset UUID (taken from the URL when that is what you have; `asset_get_by_id` needs the UUID) when there is one, what to produce, the key facts, and the instruction to fetch the asset (`asset_get_by_id`) or rebuild it from the deal, and to start with `ask_octave`. Typical prompts: draft the follow-up email for a chosen move; rehearse the call as a chosen persona using the expected concerns; go deeper on one lookalike deal; find and add the missing person; refresh the prep after the call. No transcript text and no other customer's name in a prompt that will leave the company.
- **Headless hosts.** In a chat or agent host that cannot render a page, deliver the same eight sections as Markdown with the same order and labels, then the prompts. HTML is for hosts that can render it. Do not ask the user to open an Octave screen.
