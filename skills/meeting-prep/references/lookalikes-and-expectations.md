# Lookalike deals and what to expect

Two sections rest on this method: "Deals like this: what worked, what didn't" and "What to expect, persona by persona". The promise to the rep: your team has been in this situation before, and there was a better path. Say what the record shows, no more.

## Building the lookalike set

A lookalike is a closed deal that matches this one on as many of these as the record supports, in this order: segment, buyer persona, motion (net new, upsell, displace incumbent), competitor or alternative in play, the main concern raised, deal size and stage reached.

1. **Seed with the graph.** `ask_octave`: "What worked in deals like this one, and who was involved?" seeded with the deal id and the resolved segment, persona and competitor ids. Note which deals, themes and people it names and what it says it could not establish.
2. **Resolve the cut.** Get the segment id from the deal's account, the persona ids from the attendees, and the competitor, alternative or objection ids from this account's calls (`get_event_detail` findings, `list_findings` with a `query` naming the concern, `eventFilters.opportunityIds` and an explicit `startDate`). Use only ids you saw.
3. **Count the cohort.** `compare_cohorts` with cohort A as the lookalike filter (segment, persona, and competitor or objection when present) against all other deals. Always set `startDate` far back (the default window is 14 days) and an `endDate`. Start with win rate; when the verdict is insufficient evidence, re-run with a leading outcome (`advanced_within_days` or `next_conversation_positive`) and say which outcome the figure is about. Pass `comparisonsDeclared` honestly. Report `observed` with its counts, then the verdict's strength; never "drives" or "causes". Check `acrossLinkScopes`: if the direction reverses under a stricter link scope, give both rates with their counts and say there is no stable direction; never show one rate as the answer. Cohort filters take library entities and deal fields, not employee count or title; for size and title cuts page `list_deals` (closed, by close date) and filter yourself, and say that you did.
4. **Make the set mechanical.** State the filter you applied (for example: closed, segment X, no renewals, 30 employees or fewer, this competitor) and apply it to the closed deals, keeping every deal that passes. Start narrow: `list_cohort_deals` (`side` A, with exactly the `cohortA`, `cohortB`, filters, window, `linkScope` and outcome of the `compare_cohorts` call) on the segment (and the competitor or concern when present) with `dealOutcome` won, then lost, gives the deals without paging the whole inventory; narrow by title, size or motion from each deal's row. Fall back to paging `list_deals` (once with `stageCategory` closed_won and once with closed_lost, sorted by close date, filtered with a script) when the cohort tools cannot express the cut, and say that you did. Compute every count and every sequence figure from exactly that list, never from the subset you happened to open. When you read only some of the deals' calls, say how many of the set have call records and how many of those were checked, and keep the two bases apart.
5. **Open the deals.** Take each deal in the table through the narrow tools before falling back to a topic search: its calls (`list_events` with `opportunityIds`, then `get_event_detail`), and where it has none, write "no calls linked" in its row. Use `search_call_transcripts` to find the words, never to choose the set. Take the two to three closest won and two to three closest lost deals (at most six rows in all), ranked by how many of the matching traits they share. Open each: `get_deal` (stakeholders, activity, loss reason for lost), `get_event_detail` on the calls before the deal closed, `get_deal_strategy_evidence` for which prescribed plays ran, and `search_call_transcripts` (`dealOutcome`, `attributedPersonaOIds`, `callPurposes`) for the exact words on the moment you are describing.
6. **Write sequence with counts.** For each lookalike, what happened on its calls before it moved or stalled: who joined and when, which concern came up, what the rep answered, what happened on the next call. Then the contrast across the set with its base: "In 2 of the 3 won deals with call records, the rep ran a same-data comparison before the proposal; in the 2 lost deals with call records, neither did." The base is the deals whose calls were read, with the cohort size beside it when you have it ("of 14 won and 11 lost in this segment").

## Counts from search results

A call enters a count only when you have both the buyer's concern and the rep's reply for it in front of you (a snippet that cuts off the reply does not count). Whether the reply "addressed" the concern is your reading: say "the reply moved to a customer story" and quote it, never "did not address" without the quote. A label on the set ("same use case", "same competitor") is written only when it is true of every row you show; otherwise say what the rows share.

## One unit per count

Count each deal once and each call once. A call linked to several deals at one account is one call and belongs to one deal in the count (the deal it is linked to by the deal record); sibling deals at the same account are removed from the lookalike set and treated as account history. When the same call links to many deals at one account, count the account as one situation and say how many independent situations the set holds ("9 deals at 3 accounts"). If the same call stands behind both a won and a lost deal, say the calls cannot separate those outcomes and leave it out of the contrast.

## Sequence claims need an excerpt per call

Every "in N of M calls" statement ties to one verbatim excerpt for each of the N calls, in the ledger. A call with no excerpt for that step is not counted; reduce N. Transcripts often hold only part of a call (an opening of a few minutes against a long recording): say once that the sequence comes from the part of each call that was available, and do not describe what happened later in the call. A figure that came from an unverified graph answer (a count, a quote, a customer's situation) is used only after the narrow tools confirm it. Do not write a lesson ("the one difference worth acting on") from calls you called non-patterns.

## Gate: no row without its record

A row may be written only after that deal's own row (`list_deals`) or `get_deal` was read in this run. Copy name, amount, close date, outcome and loss category from it, never from the graph answer, from memory of another deal, or from a similar-looking deal. "Asked about X" or "named competitor Y" is written only when a finding or transcript line you read for that deal says so; otherwise leave the cell as "not checked". If that gate leaves a row mostly empty, drop the row and lower the set size you state. A short true table beats a full table with guesses. The table lists every deal in the set or says it is partial ("6 of the 10 shown, the 6 with the most calls"). A statement about "all" or "N of M" deals holds only for the deals whose own record or call you read; never carry a fact from one deal to its neighbors, and never carry an account-level fact (existing customer, seniority) onto a deal that does not show it.

## Table cells: a fixed set of fields

Each lookalike row uses only these fields, each read from that deal's own record or calls: outcome and close date, segment, employee count, the CRM's loss reason in its own words, quoted (lost deals), number of calls and the span between them, titles of the people who joined, the competitor or alternative named in its findings, the concern raised (a quote or a finding you read), and what the rep did on the call you read. "What they share with this deal" lists the filter terms the deal passed, nothing more. No adjectives about a deal's use case, size or "type of buyer" that you did not read. A cell you did not check says "not checked". Quotes carry a date.

## Rules for this section

- Other deals at the same account (an earlier purchase, a lost renewal, a sibling opportunity) are account history, not lookalikes: put them in the context section and say what they change (for example, "a small deal closed won earlier this year, so confirm what was sold before pitching it again").
- Group by what the rep can act on (a play, a person added, a comparison, a sequence), not by account.
- Won and lost both appear. If only one side exists, say so.
- An account name appears only in the internal comparison. Mark the section internal.
- When no play separates the deals that moved from the ones that stalled (the same rep behavior appears on both), say exactly that: "no separating play found in the N deals with call records", and give the one thing that did differ, if any (who was on the calls, how many calls, how long between them). A flat result still tells the rep not to expect a trick.
- When the set is thin (fewer than three decided deals), say that in the first line and present what exists as individual deals, not a pattern. When none exists, say what you looked for (segment, persona, competitor) and that no decided deals matched. Do not widen the cut silently; if you widen it, say how.
- Loss reasons are the CRM's own words, quoted, and carry no diagnosis. A category from a cohort filter is normalized from the CRM text: never present it as the CRM's.
- Verification is the point: any figure that came from the graph answer alone is marked as unverified or dropped.

## What to expect, persona by persona

One block per invited guest who is likely to speak or decide, at most five (the guest list comes from the invite, see the task method step 0). Suggested roles, labeled "suggested, not confirmed", are used only when no invite was found.

1. **Who they are.** Name, title, persona and buying role as the data gives them. A derived role is labeled "likely". If the persona record exists, take the persona's stated pains and priorities from it as guidance, not as fact about this person.
2. **What this person has already said** on this account, in their words with the date. This is the strongest item.
3. **What this persona typically raises** (when no persona resolves for the attendee, use the nearest persona by buying role, label it low confidence, or write that no comparable persona was found) at this stage in this segment. Evidence: `ask_octave` for the persona (seeded with the persona id), then `search_call_transcripts` with `attributedPersonaOIds`, `attributedSegmentOIds`, `speakerSide: external` and `callPurposes` near this stage, ideally with `dealOutcome` `WON` and `LOST`; `get_entity_evidence` for the persona or its objections. Quote buyers from other accounts without their company or name, describing them only with words the record contains ("a marketing lead in this segment, Mar 12"); the internal lookalike table is the only place other accounts are named. Build any denominator with `list_events` and `includeParticipants` (call purposes, stage at the event and deal outcome in the filters, paged to the end), counting calls whose external participants map to the persona, so the base and the quote search measure the same people (a filter on events tagged with a persona is a wider group: if you use it, say the base is approximate), and open each call's findings; `search_call_transcripts` returns at most a page of best-matching moments, so it supplies quotes, never the base. If you did not enumerate the full set, write "of the N calls checked". Report the concern, the count of lookalike calls it came up on ("on 4 of the 6 calls with marketing operations leads"), and one short quote with date. Label it "likely": the evidence is other accounts.
4. **The answer that worked.** The seller's authored answer (`get_entity` on the objection or use case) and what the rep said on the lookalike deals that moved, with the count. Where no answer is authored, say so and give the question that would surface the concern.
5. **The question to ask them.** A question in their own words that would confirm or disconfirm the expected concern.

Directionally accurate is the bar. Never state an expectation as certain, never invent a quote, and never attribute a concern to a named attendee that only their persona raises elsewhere: write "people in this role tend to raise", then what this person said.
