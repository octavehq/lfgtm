# one-pager: task method

## Two ways in

**Content is supplied.** Notes, findings, or another skill's result. Do not research again. Find the one conclusion the reader should leave with and build the one-pager around it. Keep every figure, name and quote exactly as supplied, with its source.

**Only a subject is given.** A company, a deal, a period, a question. Resolve it to real records first and stop if it cannot be found. Gather evidence by [evidence and inputs](../../shared/evidence-and-inputs.md); for questions that span records, start with one focused question to the knowledge graph and verify what it cites with the narrow tools ([research toolkit](../../shared/octave-research-toolkit.md)).

In both cases, write down before outlining: who reads this, what they will do after, and the one line they would repeat to someone else.

## Narrative patterns by purpose

Pick the pattern that matches the occasion, and name it in the outline.

| Purpose | The reader must | Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Follow-up** after a call or demo | remember what was agreed and act on it | what was shown and what landed; the issue still open; the proof that answers it; the agreed next step with its date |
| **Leave-behind** for a named account | carry the case to someone who was not in the room | their problem as they stated it; the one mechanism that addresses it; one proof item; the next decision |
| **Cold outreach** | recognise their situation | the situation as a hypothesis to check; the change it would take; a comparable result; one low-cost next step |
| **POC or success plan** | agree what will be measured | the goal; the measure, its baseline and threshold; who owns what and when; the decision the result leads to |

One reader, one problem, one proof, one next step. The rules for each pattern follow.

## Every one-pager

Before outlining, select one reader, one occasion, one material problem, one offering mechanism, one suitable proof item, and one next decision. Record whether the account problem is confirmed in CRM/calls, supplied by the operator, or an ICP-based hypothesis.

## The follow-up and leave-behind patterns

For a demo follow-up, read the relevant call and CRM next step; identify what was demonstrated, the buyer's response, the unresolved issue, and agreed action. Ask only for absent material inputs: “The call confirms the reporting bottleneck and requested POC. Which next step should this document request, and is there a validated POC result missing from Octave?” Draft while awaiting that answer; populate the working field and rerun the review afterward. Read the call by [Reading Calls and Deals](../../shared/information-principles.md#reading-calls-and-deals) rules 16 to 21. On the page, a concern that praises the offering's reach is scope to address, a pain the demo left unanswered belongs on it, personal facts never reach it, and proof comes only from the record.

For a named account, check the account's history first with [Ask Octave first](../../shared/octave-research-toolkit.md#ask-octave-first-for-questions-that-span-records): one `ask_octave` question on what its calls and deals show and which proof points back comparable won deals. Use it to pick the problem, proof, and next step, and confirm each with the narrow tools; discard the answer if it resolved a different account, and drop any statement that cites another account's calls. Skip it for a persona or category page with no real account. If the tool is unavailable, use the CRM and call tools and say so.

## The cold outreach and POC patterns

For cold outreach, describe a relevant buyer situation as a hypothesis to validate. Do not assert that this account has a particular pain or installed competitor without evidence. Connect the problem to a specific capability/workflow change, then to a relevant result or honest qualitative proof. A POC planning one-pager can leave future results open when the measurement, owner, timing, and decision are explicit.

## The page

Resolve format from the request: a printable one-pager/leave-behind is one physical page; a scrolling brief is a webpage and may be longer. Ask only when the request is ambiguous. For print, choose the requested paper size or state the default, and generate the corresponding Letter or A4 layout with @page dimensions and explicit margins. Keep essential text at least 11 pt. Fit by editing priorities and shortening copy; never shrink the entire document to hide overflow. Check the actual PDF page count and render before delivery. If another paper size is promised, validate that export too.

Choose proof supported by the source: a measured transformation with period/units, an approved attributable quote, or a concrete qualitative workflow/result. One strong relevant item is sufficient; no metric is required when none is available. Never add a second number merely to match a layout. Download the validated logo/icon into the selected asset bundle or embed it, verify its identity and use status, and check offline rendering. Do not rely on a third-party favicon hotlink in an output described as self-contained.
