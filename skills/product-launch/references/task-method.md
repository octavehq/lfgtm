# product-launch: task method

Load [gtm-context.md](../../shared/gtm-context.md); resolve the launch entity and parent offering,
Motion, and audience cells. A feature may be linked to a product/service/
solution; do not assume the launch itself is a Product.

Retrieve current offering details, release resources, and relevant CRM/call
signals. Build a launch manifest: what changes from the current experience;
capability and limits; beta/GA/planned state; availability date and timezone;
entitlement/plan, geography, pricing, prerequisites, migration/upgrade,
rollout dependencies, support path, approved proof, and primary destination.
Each fact records source, scope, status (confirmed/planned/embargoed/unknown),
and which requested assets depend on it.

Load [evidence-and-inputs.md](../../shared/evidence-and-inputs.md) for material gaps. Group questions:
1. Release: what is available to whom, when, and with what prerequisites?
2. Commercial/operational: price, entitlement, migration, support, limits?
3. Evidence: what customer/POC result substantiates the proposed benefit,
   under what conditions, and may it be used externally?
Ask only unanswered material items. Incorporate operator replies with their
source and scope; reconcile conflicts before copy generation. Retain the
manifest between turns. Unknowns can become agreed working placeholders,
qualitative language, or a future-measurement task with owner and event.

Create a row for each relevant segment × persona × Motion/lifecycle:
eligibility; problem/trigger; changed workflow; differentiated benefit;
proof; objection; offer/CTA; channel; owner; timing/dependency; success event.
Explain priority using benefit, reachable eligible audience, urgency, and
readiness. Separate customer adoption, expansion, new acquisition, free-to-
paid conversion, and partner enablement when relevant.

Choose launch tier and timeline from release readiness and commercial
objective. Schedule enablement before the audience it supports; honor
notice/migration constraints. For each channel, specify its audience, asset,
destination, owner, and reason to use it. Do not create a customer story
on a fixed week unless evidence and permission will actually exist.

Define success separately for delivery/reach, qualified demand, adoption,
and business outcome. Record baseline, denominator, measurement window,
source/owner, and review decision. Ask for supplementary telemetry where
needed; an unknown baseline can be an instrumentation task, not a number.

Give every generation call the same launch-manifest version, approved
message hierarchy, applicable audience row, claim constraints, and draft/
final status. Use existing generation tools and their available parameters.
Generate the requested assets fully:
- Customer email: eligibility, change, benefit, required action, timing.
- Prospect email: relevant pain/trigger, new reason to act, credible CTA.
- Blog: problem, mechanism, capability delta, limits/proof, next step.
- Social set: distinct announcement, problem, demo, and use-case angles.
- Sales one-pager: who fits, discovery, talk track, objections, proof, CTA.
- FAQ: actual entitlement, price, rollout, migration, and support answers.
- Competitive talking points, when applicable: changed buyer criterion,
  sourced comparison, legitimate limitation, and discovery question.

Save an asset manifest with audience, file/section, fact-version, owner,
status, and unresolved dependencies. Load [output-readiness.md](../../shared/output-readiness.md) at
delivery. Read every requested asset back; reconcile dates, eligibility,
price, naming, links, and CTA. A draft kit can contain agreed placeholders;
check off an asset only when its substantive draft/final content exists.
Continue through operator corrections and regenerate affected assets.
