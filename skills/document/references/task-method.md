## Two ways in

**Content is supplied.** Notes, findings, a transcript, or another skill's result. Do not research again. Read all of it, find the one conclusion the reader should leave with, and build the document around that. Keep every figure, name and quote exactly as supplied, with its source. If the content does not support a conclusion, say what it does support and ask for the missing piece.

**Only a subject is given.** A company, a deal, a competitor, a period, a question. Resolve it to real records first (the account, the deal, the calls, the library entities) and stop if it cannot be found. Gather evidence by [evidence and inputs](../../shared/evidence-and-inputs.md): for questions that span records, start with one focused question to the knowledge graph and verify what it cites with the narrow tools ([research toolkit](../../shared/octave-research-toolkit.md)). Read calls and deals by the rules in [information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md#reading-calls-and-deals).

In both cases, write down before outlining: who reads this, what they will do after reading it, and the one line they would repeat to someone else. Every section exists to support that line.

## Narrative patterns by purpose

Pick the pattern that matches what the reader has to do. Name it in the outline.

| Purpose | The reader must | Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decision** | choose between options | the recommendation; the options side by side on the same criteria; the evidence for the recommended one; what it costs and risks; the decision asked for and by when |
| **Findings** | understand what is true | the headline finding; each finding as a claim with its evidence, strongest first; what it changes; what to do about it |
| **Plan** | carry something out | the outcome and its date; the steps in order with owners; what each step depends on; how progress is checked |
| **Review** | learn from what happened | what was expected against what happened; why, with evidence; what to keep; what to change, each as an action |

A document that mixes two patterns reads as two documents. If the request needs both, make the second a short closing section, or make two.

## What every section needs

- **A claim as its heading.** The heading says what was found or what to do, with its number when it has one. A topic label ("Competitive landscape") is not a heading.
- **The evidence that carries the claim.** A dated quote with who said it, a figure with what it counts and over what period, a named deal or account. One strong piece beats three weak ones.
- **One job.** If a section needs two headings to describe it, it is two sections.
- **Length that matches its weight.** The section that carries the decision gets the room. Background gets a few lines.
- **A table for anything compared.** Options, periods, segments or competitors on the same attributes go in a table, with the attribute names in plain words.

## What to cut

- Anything the reader already knows about their own company or product.
- Method: how the evidence was found, what was searched, what could not be checked.
- A second example that makes the same point as the first.
- Any section whose removal the reader would not notice.

When the content is longer than the format holds, cut whole sections from the end of the priority order. Never shrink the type or crowd a section to fit.

## Before delivery

Read the finished document as its reader. Check that the first screen says what this is and what to do, that each claim is backed where it is made, that nothing is said twice, and that it ends on a next step with an owner and a date the record supports. Then run the format's review checklist and [output readiness](../../shared/output-readiness.md).
