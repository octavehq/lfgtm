## Two ways in

**Content is supplied.** Notes, findings, a transcript, or another skill's result. Do not research again. Read all of it, find the one conclusion the reader should leave with, and build the magazine around that. Keep every figure, name and quote exactly as supplied, with its source. If the content does not support a conclusion, say what it does support and ask for the missing piece.

**Only a subject is given.** A company, a deal, a competitor, a period, a question. Resolve it to real records first (the account, the deal, the calls, the library entities) and stop if it cannot be found. Gather evidence by [evidence and inputs](../../shared/evidence-and-inputs.md): for questions that span records, start with one focused question to the knowledge graph and verify what it cites with the narrow tools ([research toolkit](../../shared/octave-research-toolkit.md)). Read calls and deals by the rules in [information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md#reading-calls-and-deals).

In both cases, write down before outlining: who reads this, what they will do after reading it, and the one line they would repeat to someone else. Every spread exists to support that line.

## Narrative patterns by purpose

Pick the pattern that matches what the reader has to do. Name it in the outline.

| Purpose | The reader must | Order of spreads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Period recap** | know what changed and why it matters | cover with the window and the headline; contents; the biggest change with its numbers; two to four stories behind the numbers, each with a voice; what to watch next; close |
| **Theme** | see a pattern across accounts or calls | cover with the claim; the pattern in one figure; three or four accounts or voices that show it, one per spread; the exception that tests it; what to do; close |
| **One story** | follow an account or deal from start to now | cover; where it began; the turning points in order, each dated; the people; where it stands; the next move; close |

A magazine that mixes two patterns reads as two documents. If the request needs both, make the second a short closing section, or make two.

## What every spread needs

- **A claim as its heading.** The heading says what was found or what to do, with its number when it has one. A topic label ("Competitive landscape") is not a heading.
- **The evidence that carries the claim.** A dated quote with who said it, a figure with what it counts and over what period, a named deal or account. One strong piece beats three weak ones.
- **One job.** If a spread needs two headings to describe it, it is two spreads.
- **A voice.** Most analytical spreads carry a person's own words, with who said them and when. A magazine with no voices is a deck.
- **One dominant element.** A figure, a quote, a chart or an image leads the spread and the text supports it. Two equal elements compete.
- **Its own surface.** Vary light and dark spreads on purpose, and never let two spreads in a row use the same composition unless they are a deliberate pair.

## What to cut

- Anything the reader already knows about their own company or product.
- Method: how the evidence was found, what was searched, what could not be checked.
- A second example that makes the same point as the first.
- Any spread whose removal the reader would not notice.

When the content is longer than the format holds, cut whole spreads from the end of the priority order. Never shrink the type or crowd a spread to fit.

## Before delivery

Read the finished magazine as its reader. Check that the first screen says what this is and what to do, that each claim is backed where it is made, that nothing is said twice, and that it ends on a next step with an owner and a date the record supports. Then run the format's review checklist and [output readiness](../../shared/output-readiness.md).
