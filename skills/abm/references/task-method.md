# abm: task method

### One company or a cohort
A request that names one company follows the account plan contract below. A request that names a segment, a cohort or a list of accounts follows [cohort-plan.md](cohort-plan.md) instead and does not build a committee for any single account. When a request could be read either way (one parent company with many divisions, a list of one), ask once and continue with the reading the wording supports.

### Account plan contract
Read [gtm-context.md](../../shared/gtm-context.md) for the selected account's offering, motion, segment, and persona cells. Read [evidence-and-inputs.md](../../shared/evidence-and-inputs.md) for claims and operator inputs; use [output-readiness.md](../../shared/output-readiness.md) for the final plan. Load HTML/brand references only when rendering the plan. Do not load mutation guidance unless the user requests a separate library change.

Retrieve CRM relationship, open opportunities, owners, activities, and relevant calls first. A lookup that errors is not "no history": retry it once, and if it still fails say the CRM could not be read. Contacts and associations are coverage, not engagement: only logged two-way communication counts as engagement. Distinguish acquisition, expansion, and displacement. Use account-wide evidence for account planning while identifying the opportunity behind any deal-specific assertion. Ask a grouped clarification for materially ambiguous offering, division, account objective, or unrecorded engagement; continue supported research and incorporate the response with provenance.

Use [Ask Octave first](../../shared/octave-research-toolkit.md#ask-octave-first-for-questions-that-span-records) for the first pass on the account: ask `ask_octave` which people at the account have appeared in calls and deals, what the account has raised, and which comparable accounts and proof points sit behind won deals for the same personas. Use that to find candidates and routes, then verify identity, participation, and role with the narrow CRM, call, and person tools. Graph links do not establish a buying role, and an answer can be wrong about this account, so drop any statement that cites another account. If the tool is unavailable, continue with the narrow tools and note it.

On an Octave host, find people at the account with `find_person`, take firmographics from `enrich_company`, and read account fit from `qualify_company`. A fit result is a score out of 10 with its reasons. Take the verdict from the qualification itself when it gives one; when it gives only a score, 7 or more is a fit unless a disqualifier was triggered, and the page says that 7 was assumed.

### What happened before
Classify the history before writing anything else, since it decides how every email opens: a lost or stalled evaluation, earlier two-way contact with no evaluation, outreach that was never answered, or none. For the first two, write two to six lines a rep reads in ten seconds: when and around what they evaluated, their situation as they described it on calls (their systems, their team, the problem in their words), what held them back, and where it ended, naming people with their titles. Add up to four lines quoted from the calls, the ones that best show why they engaged, each with who said it, and the main objection someone there raised with how the team answered it. Only text read in a transcript goes inside quotes. For unanswered outreach, list at most five touches with date, channel, sender and recipient. With none, say so in one line.

### Buying signals and why now
Give one verdict per buying trigger the workspace defines. A hit needs public evidence about this company from the last 24 months: evidence about a namesake, a parent or a subsidiary is discarded, not downgraded, and the company marketing its own AI or automation is not evidence of an initiative inside it. Each hit carries the date its source shows (the full day when shown, never invented), the page it is on, a short label for that page, and one or two sentences a rep could repeat. The company's own announcement beats a secondhand report.

A signal can be the reason to reach out now only when it is dated and no more than about four months old; a funding round is never the reason on its own. Older hits stay on the plan as context and are labelled as older. With no signal that qualifies, say that the plan is proactive and lean on fit.

### The committee
People the team has already spoken with come first, in this order: people from the account who spoke on calls, people in email threads with the team, the account's other CRM contacts, and then a search per persona for the roles still open. Check each person from the first three groups against a current profile (`enrich_person`), use the current title, and drop anyone who has left the account.

Select the committee from this customer's relevant persona cells. For each person, record identity source, participation evidence, and buying-role evidence independently. A verified title without engagement supports a candidate, not a confirmed champion. A seller-reported role is usable with that provenance. Preserve role gaps instead of manufacturing names. Count a role as missing only when it should be engaged at this account's stage, size and motion, list later roles as needed later, and write the committee as people, never seats ([information principles](../../shared/information-principles.md#reading-calls-and-deals) rules 23 and 24; [editorial rule 14b](../../shared/editorial-rules.md)).

For every buying trigger, separate the library definition from the account occurrence. Mark occurrence Known only when dated account evidence or attributed operator confirmation establishes it. Otherwise show Trigger to validate, its relevance, and a confirming question. Apply the same separation to persona pains: strategic relevance is not confirmed account pain.

Build the engagement plan before rendering: entry person or role, why this entry point, supported hook, the outreach, and the sequence for involving the other people on the committee. For each person on the primary committee write one line on what this role at this company worries about, two or three sentences on how to frame the product for this person, and the opening move (the strongest signal or engagement fact with its numbers, then the angle tied to what they own). If a line would read equally true at another company, rewrite it. Full depth writes an email and a three-beat talk track for each of those people; Quick writes them for the entry person. Both follow [outreach copy](outreach-copy.md). The hook is a signal that qualifies as a reason now or, with none, a fit reason, and never an old signal dressed as news.

The plan itself is one or two sentences on the sequencing approach, then three to five steps. Each step is one action by the rep to one named person on the committee over one channel (email, call or LinkedIn), saying what to send or say and why, spelled out so the rep can act without scrolling back. "Email the CEO" and "follow up in two weeks" are not steps. Base each touch on prior CRM/call context, the recipient's cell, relevant proof, and the next desired commitment. State the condition for the next step rather than assuming each touch succeeds. Fit the plan to the account's setting per [Reading Calls and Deals](../../shared/information-principles.md#reading-calls-and-deals) rules 16 to 21. Personal facts stay out of the plan, the emails and the risks, and a relationship that changes how the account is run appears once in the internal plan's risks and never in the emails. Pair each material risk with its mitigation.

Every fact the plan, an email or a talk track relies on is visible on the page: a signal, a line of history, or an entry in the research used with its source. A number a rep is told to say is never untraceable.

Keep the committee, per-persona positioning, outreach, and sequence aligned. For every selected persona provide concerns, current approach, lead argument, relevant proof or an honest proof gap, and desired next action. Review the complete plan including outreach; deliver only that reviewed version. A later content change requires checking the changed claims and final artifact again.

### Depth and scope
Quick: a compact account decision, the strongest available entry route, up to three primary people/roles, relevant persona framing, one sample email, and the next three actions. Retrieve enough evidence for those claims; put unresolved research into a short next-check list.

Full: cover the requested stakeholder count and relevant buying roles, all material persona framing, engagement sequence, and risks. Show a compact primary committee with additional verified people in an appendix when needed. The user's explicit count overrides default display caps. Return an honest discovery shortfall rather than weak matches or invented people. Do not treat a fixed security persona triad as the default committee.

## Data beside the document

When the run is asked for data beside its document (the skill's `output.schema.json`), return the account's name and domain, the one-sentence reason to reach out now in `whyNow` (null when no signal qualifies), each buying signal as a row of `signals` (what happened, its date, and the page it is on), each mapped person as a row of `stakeholders` (name, title, matched persona, their part in a decision, how the team knows them in `source`, profile link, and one sentence on the angle) and each recommended play as a row of `plays` in the order to run them. Copy names, titles and links exactly as a tool returned them, write null for what no tool returned, and use `unknown` for a part in a decision the evidence does not show. Set `scope` to `account`.

For a cohort plan, set `scope` to `cohort`, leave `accountName` null and `stakeholders` empty, set `whyNow` to null and `signals` to empty, and return the cohort in `cohort` (its name, the angle it shares, and each member account with its tier and why it belongs) and each play as a row of `plays` with its `tier`. Name no person for an individual account: those come from the plan for each account.
