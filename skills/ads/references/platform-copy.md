# ads: platform copy

How ad copy differs by platform. Read this before writing any headline, description or primary text, and write each platform's copy for that platform. Copy shortened from another platform's version reads as what it is.

## Contents

- Rules for every platform
- Google Search (responsive search ads)
- LinkedIn (single image and document ads)
- Meta (Facebook and Instagram feed)
- Before export

## Rules for every platform

- **One source card, several executions.** The claim, mechanism and proof come from the source card. The wording is rewritten for each platform's reader and field lengths.
- **Lengths below are what shows without being cut off**, taken from each platform's own ad specifications. The hard maximums are higher and change; check the platform's current specification before export and note the date you checked.
- **Count characters with spaces and punctuation.** Report the count beside every field.
- **The buyer's words beat ours.** When a source card holds a phrase a buyer used, try it in the field before a phrase from the messaging.
- **No caveat inside a field.** A claim that needs one is reworded or dropped (task method).

## Google Search (responsive search ads)

The reader typed a query. Answer it.

| Field | Length | How many |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | 30 characters | 3 to 15 |
| Description | 90 characters | 2 to 4 |
| Path | 15 characters each | up to 2 |

- **Any headline can appear with any other, in any order.** Each must make sense alone and beside the others. Do not write a headline that finishes another's sentence.
- **Cover different jobs across the set:** the query's own words, the outcome, the mechanism, proof, the offer, the call to action. Fifteen rewordings of one idea give the platform nothing to choose between.
- **Put the keyword theme in at least two headlines** in the words a searcher uses, not the category name we prefer.
- **A keyword too long for a headline goes in a description.**
- **Pin sparingly.** Pin only what must show (a brand name, a required disclosure); every pin removes combinations.
- **Descriptions carry the mechanism and proof** the headline has no room for. One complete thought each.

## LinkedIn (single image and document ads)

The reader is at work, scrolling past colleagues' posts. They did not ask.

| Field | Length that shows in full |
| --- | --- |
| Introductory text | about 150 characters before "see more" |
| Headline | about 70 characters |

- **The first line of the introductory text is the ad.** Lead with the reader's situation or a specific finding, in the words of their role. The company name can wait.
- **Write to one role.** Targeting is by title and function, so the copy can be that specific: name the job, the tension in it, the decision in front of them.
- **The headline says what they get by clicking** (the guide, the benchmark, the demo), not the product's tagline.
- **Peer proof outperforms claims here:** a named role at a comparable company, a figure with its unit and period.
- **Plain and professional.** No clickbait openers, no stacked emoji, no questions the reader would answer "no" to.
- **A longer introductory text is allowed and is cut off.** Use the extra room only when the first 150 characters already stand alone.

## Meta (Facebook and Instagram feed)

The reader is off the clock. The image or video does most of the work and the copy supports it.

| Field | Length that shows in full |
| --- | --- |
| Primary text | about 125 characters (keep it to two or three lines) |
| Headline | about 27 to 40 characters, by ad format |
| Link description | about 25 characters, where it shows |

- **Say one thing.** Problem or outcome in the first sentence, in everyday words. Business jargon reads as an ad from the first word.
- **Fields show differently by placement.** In the Instagram feed the primary text shows and the headline may not; in Stories the reverse. Each field must work without the other.
- **Match the creative.** The first words should name what the image shows or complete its thought.
- **The headline is the action or the offer**, short enough to read at a glance.

## Before export

For every variant, check: the count is inside the length for its platform; it stands alone; it traces to a source card; and no two variants on the same platform say the same thing in different words. A variant that fails is rewritten, not trimmed.
